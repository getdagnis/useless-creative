# USELESS® — medium-term roadmap (draft)

_19 September 2026 · Working proposal, to revise after the visual investigation_

## Direction

Build USELESS® as a recognizable creative world. The first public proof is **RED / Series 01**: physical stickers placed where official-looking messages acquire an unexpected meaning. The Kickstarter is a first release and test of demand, not the limit of the brand.

The near-term order is **recover the strongest work → invite collaborators → shape the first release → prove production and participation → make a launch decision**. The stages are decision gates, not calendar promises. Useful work can overlap where it does not depend on an unresolved choice.

## 1. Recover the project and choose its strongest evidence

Continue the current pass through exported visuals, video, audio, scripts and product sketches. Select a compact set that shows the concept, the visual system, real-world placements, possible extensions and work already made. Keep a separate record of material that is promising, obsolete or unresolved; do not treat an old render, script or campaign draft as a commitment.

**Outcome:** A small, legible visual selection and a one-page account of what USELESS® is, what its first release is, and which choices remain open.

**Gate:** An unfamiliar creative person can understand the idea from the selected material without seeing the entire archive. The audit may continue afterward, but new discoveries should have to earn their place.

## 2. Make the collaborator invitation

Create a short, visually led presentation for a deliberately small group of potential creative and operational collaborators. Show the cultural tension, the seriousness of the visual language, stickers working in context, the physical first release, and enough of the wider USELESS® world to invite contribution. Make the ask concrete: what is already there, what needs another person, and what joining could mean. Discuss actual roles and terms directly with each person rather than implying a settled team structure in the deck.

**Outcome:** A sendable deck and a short list of people with a clear reason to approach each one.

**Gate:** The deck generates useful conversations: people can describe the idea back, identify where they could contribute, and raise questions worth answering. Keep progressing if no one joins.

## 3. Define RED / Series 01 as a credible product and campaign

Use the collaborator conversations and the recovered material to refine the first collection, its placement imagery, the campaign thesis and the desired emotional progression: contemporary human/automation tension → USELESS as serious-looking urban intervention → a tangible product → controlled absurd escalation. Rework the video from its intent and source material; the old lighthearted render is a reference, not the target. Develop the Kickstarter page, website and video as one story with different jobs.

In parallel, check the physical offer early: production methods, packaging options, costs, fulfillment and reward logic. The large box, smaller box and foil package are working concepts; sticker counts and tiers stay provisional until those checks support them. Keep the immediate product intelligible even when showing larger ambitions.

**Outcome:** One agreed campaign narrative, a coherent RED product proposal, a short list of campaign images and a credible production range.

**Gate:** Someone can grasp why the object is desirable, what they would receive and why this world extends beyond stickers. The offer can be produced at plausible quantities and prices.

## 4. Prove participation and the public-facing story

Resolve the simplest satisfying vote: see a sticker, judge it, see a response, move on. Give voting a real connection to the eventual selection without presenting rankings as the sole editorial authority. Build only the website and data support required to test that loop, explain the product and direct people toward the campaign. Test the story with people outside the project as well as creative peers; observe confusion, delight, repeat voting and actual purchase interest separately.

**Outcome:** A usable campaign-facing experience, a tested page/video narrative and a defensible selection approach for the first print run.

**Gate:** Visitors understand the physical product and participation quickly; the core flow works reliably; feedback gives a reason to believe people may back it. Repair weak explanation or product evidence before adding features.

## 5. Make the Kickstarter launch decision

Lock the first edition only when final samples or reliable production proofs, costs, delivery assumptions, reward clarity, campaign page, video, candidate pool and voting experience support the same promise. Establish a realistic funding goal and fulfillment plan from quotes, then decide whether to launch, narrow the offer or run another focused test.

**Outcome:** A launch-ready first campaign with known costs and owners for the remaining work, or a written reason to adjust the release.

**Gate:** A backer can tell what they will get, when, and how their support helps make it real; the project can plausibly deliver it.

## Keep visible, without turning it into current scope

- The USELESS® world can support other formats, fictional institutions and public interventions. Preserve strong findings from the audit as future creative territory; do not turn them into a product schedule yet.
- Archive texts, stretch goals, pins, nameplates and other artifacts are evidence of range. Revive an idea only when it strengthens the collaborator invitation or the first release.
- Exact sticker counts, voting rules, backend choices and video format are working decisions. Set them when the product, experience and production evidence justify them.

## Next move

Finish the current exported-material investigation, then assemble the deck selection. Update this roadmap at that gate before committing to campaign production.
